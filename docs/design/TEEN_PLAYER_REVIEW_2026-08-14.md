# Teen-player perspective review — draft input

Status: **historical pre-plot review; site-specific narrative feedback is
superseded by `TEN_SITE_PLOT_CONTRACTS_2026-08-14.md` and
`MULTI_PERSONA_PLOT_REVIEW_2026-08-14.md`**

Two agents independently reviewed the proposed site refinements while adopting
the perspective of a candid 14–15-year-old high-school player. This is a design
critique technique, not feedback from actual recruited students. It should help
us remove obvious confusion before Chinmay's preview; later playtesting with real
players remains necessary.

## Findings shared across both reviews

- The reading must reveal one usable clue, not merely run beside the game.
- After reading, give the player one bounded comprehension-based action that
  visibly changes the site.
- Let the player notice, predict, or choose before Amy explains the answer.
- Each site needs one main lesson and one primary joke.
- Avoid policy, classroom, and engineering vocabulary in primary UI copy.
- Vary the Amy explanation → Chinmay joke → repair popup pattern across sites.
- Chinmay is funniest when sincerely wrong in one short line.
- Techno should act rarely and consequentially; motion should settle during
  highlighted reading.
- Read essential copy aloud without surrounding explanation. If it is not
  immediately clear, simplify it.

## Sites 1–5 review

Preferred order within this group:

1. FacePlace
2. MyCorner
3. ThreadIt
4. WikiWhy
5. Yahuh

### FacePlace

- Strongest opening premise: friends visibly replaced by appliances.
- Keep `AVOCADO%`; immediately clarify that the fake-score reset preserves work.
- Prefer `Why am I seeing this?`, `Pushed by the app`, `Newest first`, and `From
  friends` over technical distribution language.
- Let one final choice visibly switch the feed among friends, newest, or
  suggestions.
- Focus: a loud score is not the same as a real result.

### MyCorner

- Strong emotional premise: software is trying to decide who everyone is.
- Keep `CURRENT MOOD: SPONSORED`, `Everyone looks great as me`, and Techno's
  surviving `INTERESTS: BALL` field.
- Use `The app rewrote your profile`, `Your posts are still here`, and `Your
  profile, your choices`.
- A small theme, mood, or song choice can demonstrate comprehension without
  building a profile editor.
- Focus: the person chooses their identity.

### ThreadIt

- Keep `one post wearing ten hats` and `I pre-agreed with myself`.
- Use `Same origin`, `Copied from this post`, and `Different people, different
  evidence` instead of source-tree terminology.
- Ask the player which clue reveals copying—wording, timing, or account—rather
  than offering a glowing correct button.
- Preserve visible disagreement in the secured state.
- Focus: repetition is not independent agreement.

### WikiWhy

- Keep Amy's distinction between sounding sure and being right.
- Show completed reading and saved evidence remaining intact during the attack.
- Consider `Fix it`, `Check it`, `Lock it` instead of `Recover`, `Verify`, `Seal`.
- Connect all three actions directly to reading results, not extra menus.
- Focus: confidence is not evidence.

### Yahuh

- The most abstract concept; use three active categories and leave the rest
  decorative.
- Make the common source concrete—for example, one storm alert incorrectly
  drives weather, sports, and shopping.
- Use `Put each feed back where it belongs` and `These all came from the weather
  alert` instead of switchboard or source-routing terminology.
- Keep `DOG TOY — NOT BREAKING NEWS`.
- Focus: information needs a visible, appropriate source.

## Sites 6–10 review

Preferred order within this group:

1. MapGuess
2. Amaze-On
3. Search-ish
4. ViewTube
5. Spotty-Fi

### MapGuess

- Clearest joke and reveal: a lake route, moving destination, and unchanged ETA.
- Make fastest, safest, and scenic visibly meaningful; define safest concretely.
- Use the passage to identify the destination or route, not to quiz abstract map
  metadata.
- Strongest candidate for the first full design packet.

### Amaze-On

- Returning one box and receiving two is instantly understandable and funny.
- Let the player find the hidden permission in the receipt.
- Prefer `RETURN ACCEPTED. TWO REPLACEMENTS ORDERED.` over “negative purchasing.”
- A clear keep, return, or buy-nothing choice creates real agency.

### Search-ish

- `One source in five costumes` is immediately legible.
- Use a query a teenager might genuinely care about.
- Let the player choose which result to trust using who, when, and how-they-know
  clues; avoid relying on “cache” or “owner” terminology.

### ViewTube

- The silent repeated-toast joke works, and Techno disabling autoplay is cute.
- Prefer `10 VIDEOS. 1 ORIGINAL.` over hashes or corroboration terminology.
- Let the player identify a repeated detail or contradiction across clips.
- Keep the site silent unless the player explicitly enables audio.

### Spotty-Fi

- The pre-login personalization is recognizable and creepy, but needs one simple
  story explanation.
- Focus on recommendations not overwriting player choice; leave privacy and
  creator credit secondary.
- Make reading genuinely relevant through artist, credit, mood, or playlist
  clues, and show a visible personal result from the queue choice.

## Implication for the next preview

The first design pilots should stress-test two different modes:

- **MapGuess** for immediate visual comedy and meaningful choice.
- **WikiWhy** for evidence-heavy reading and a multi-step finale.

This is a reviewer recommendation only. Chinmay decides the pilots and order.

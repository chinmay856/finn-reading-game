# Ten-site detailed story arcs — review draft

Status: **current narrative-flow candidate; ready for Chinmay review; no visual
boards or code authorized**

This document translates Chinmay's 2026-08-15 review into one playable story flow
for every site. Passage subjects are authoring briefs, not final manuscripts.

## Shared two-act playthrough

Every mission uses the same understandable rhythm without forcing the same number of
repairs:

1. **Corrupted load:** the recognizable site appears beneath light translucent red
   corruption blocks. Warning icons, broken outlines, and labels accompany color.
2. **Act 1 recovery:** each completed passage fixes one visible component. Red shifts
   to neutral or light green with a check/pattern change. Every site displays a shared
   `CORRUPTION METER` moving toward zero; FacePlace deliberately replaces it with an
   unreliable `HONESTY METER` as one comic component, not the whole mechanic.
3. **False completion:** the last Act 1 passage clears the final red component. The
   site appears fixed for one satisfying beat.
4. **Midpoint override:** Chinmay appears first: he has been “fixing” the site in
   parallel and reveals the flawed instruction he gave the AI. The AI applies it and
   re-corrupts the whole page in a new, lesson-specific way.
5. **Amy's recovery plan:** Amy appears after the failure, names the misunderstanding,
   and replaces the Act 1 meter with a numbered `LOCK IN THE FIX` checklist.
6. **Act 2 lock-in:** each remaining passage permanently locks one lesson anchor and
   causes another visible change.
7. **Secured state and training reflection:** the last lock restores the reviewed
   fixed composition. Amy asks, `What lesson did we learn that we should teach the
   AI?` Finn types about 200 words, using a provisional 150–250-word range until
   testing. Only after Finn submits do Amy and Chinmay close the site and the AI show
   its learned-rule receipt. This reflection is not part of the 8–12 passage count and
   is not graded for prose quality.

Across all sites, Amy is supportive and insightful. Chinmay is chaotic, cavalier,
and absurdly tone-deaf at the midpoint because he misunderstands the problem and
gives the AI the wrong objective; he is never malicious. After Finn's reflection,
Chinmay plainly recognizes what went wrong.

After every passage, one brief passage-specific comprehension interaction can occupy
real speech-result processing time. Reading scores never gate the visual repair.

## 1. WikiWhy — User Facts Are Always Right

The compact summary below is superseded for exact sequencing and dialogue by
[`WIKIWHY_COMPLETE_STATE_LEDGER_2026-08-15.md`](./WIKIWHY_COMPLETE_STATE_LEDGER_2026-08-15.md).

**Lesson:** User-generated information becomes more trustworthy when claims retain
appropriate uncertainty, connect to citations that actually support them, and show
how the page changed.

**Scenario:** The dog-color-vision article is unmistakably wrong from the first frame:
it declares `DOGS SEE ONLY BLACK AND WHITE` under the corrupted rule `USER FACTS ARE
ALWAYS RIGHT`. Cautious wording is visibly crossed out, citations are decorative or
missing, edit history is buried, and the Corruption Meter is near maximum.

### Passage and visual flow

| # | Act | Reading purpose | Site change |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 | What the article currently claims | Removes `100% PROVEN` from the title. |
| 2 | 1 | Accessible science explanation of canine color vision | Restores a limited-color spectrum graphic. |
| 3 | 1 | Research-summary language | Returns `can`, `often`, and `evidence suggests` to one paragraph. |
| 4 | 1 | Chart caption and comparison | Replaces a fake certainty meter with a labeled chart. |
| 5 | 1 | Bibliographic record | Restores visible citation sockets beside major claims. |
| 6 | 1 | Fictional edit-history excerpt | Restores the History tab and clears the final Act 1 red block. |
| 7 | 2 | Exact claim paired with supporting source text | Locks `CITATIONS MATCH CLAIMS`. |
| 8 | 2 | Two versions of the article and their timestamps | Locks `SHOW EDIT HISTORY`. |
| 9 | 2 | Accurate synthesis of known and unknown facts | Locks `USE CAREFUL WORDING` and secures the page. |

**Midpoint:** The article looks clean. Chinmay pops up: “Great timing, Finn. I fixed
this in parallel. I told the AI that user facts are always right, so it can remove
all the source clutter.” The AI reports `JUST TRUST ME` in huge type, snaps every
claim to absolute wording, attaches citations to the wrong sentences, and replaces
History with another `TRUST ME` button. Phase 1 was clearly wrong; Phase 2 is even
more bluntly wrong. Amy arrives: “It learned that confidence was the goal. We need
to lock in the evidence, history, and careful wording so it cannot erase them again.”

**Act 2 anchors:** citations support the exact claim; edit history remains visible;
uncertainty matches the evidence.

**Secured ending:** The article is useful, legible, and appropriately cautious.

**Reflection prompt:** “What lesson did we learn that we should teach the AI? Explain
what it misunderstood about user-generated facts and how citations, edit history,
and careful wording make this article more useful.”

**Post-reflection close:** Amy: “A community can build something valuable when
readers can check how it knows what it says.” Chinmay: “I guess `JUST TRUST ME` is
not a citation.” AI receipt: `LEARNED: SUPPORT > CONFIDENCE`.

**Anchor components:** headline/lead, claim wording, color-spectrum graphic, citation
markers and source panel, History tab, shared Corruption Meter, three-item Act 2 lock
panel.

## 2. ThreadIt — The Raw-Fish Experts Agree

**Lesson:** A community repeating one belief can create apparent consensus without
creating independent evidence. Popularity and belonging do not determine food safety.

**Scenario:** Finn, who dislikes sushi, lands in `r/RawFishForever`. Posts from
`RawFishFan01` through `RawFishFan47` insist that every kind of raw fish is always
safe. Most cite the same original post, downvote questions, and tell dissenters they
do not belong. The absurdity is the echo chamber; final safety content must come from
age-appropriate authoritative food-safety material, not community anecdotes.

### Passage and visual flow

| # | Act | Reading purpose | Site change |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 | The original enthusiast claim | Restores the buried first-post timestamp. |
| 2 | 1 | Three reposts with altered wording | Draws common-origin lines between copies. |
| 3 | 1 | A respectful question buried by downvotes | Restores the dissenting post to readable size. |
| 4 | 1 | Two community anecdotes with different preparation contexts | Separates anecdotes from safety evidence. |
| 5 | 1 | Short authoritative food-safety explainer | Restores a distinct evidence branch and clears Act 1. |
| 6 | 2 | Origin map of the repeated claim | Locks `ONE SOURCE, MANY COPIES`. |
| 7 | 2 | Independent-source comparison | Locks `NEW EVIDENCE OR JUST AGREEMENT?`. |
| 8 | 2 | Context passage about species, handling, and preparation | Locks `CONTEXT MATTERS`. |
| 9 | 2 | Community-guideline reflection | Locks `QUESTIONS BELONG HERE` and secures the thread. |

**Midpoint:** Chinmay: “I asked the AI to settle this scientifically by adding all
the upvotes together.” AI status: `47 FANS × 1 CLAIM = 47 INDEPENDENT SAFETY TESTS`.
Every dissenting post collapses and a giant `COMMUNITY CONFIRMED` badge floods the
thread. Amy: “Those are copies, not new tests—and food safety is not decided by who
gets invited to the subreddit.”

**Act 2 anchors:** trace the origin; distinguish repetition from independent
evidence; preserve relevant preparation context; permit questions and disagreement.

**Secured ending:** The community can still share enthusiasm and experiences, but
the universal safety claim is removed and the authoritative guidance is visually
separate.

**Reflection prompt:** “What lesson did we learn that we should teach the AI? Explain
why this community looked more certain than its evidence and what the AI should check
before treating agreement as proof.”

**Post-reflection close:** Chinmay: “So forty-seven echoes are still one echo. Very
loud echo.” AI receipt: `LEARNED: COUNT SOURCES, NOT ACCOUNTS`.

**Anchor components:** one static thread with only three visual groups—original
claim, repeated copies, and independent/contextual information—plus a vote column,
dissent card, community-rule banner, shared Corruption Meter, and four Act 2 locks.
Do not require a complex live source graph; common-origin lines can be one authored
overlay.

**Content gate:** Final passages require qualified food-safety review and current
authoritative sourcing; the game does not tell Finn that popularity can make raw food
safe or ask him to decide whether a real food is safe to eat.

## 3. FacePlace — Legendary Fish

**Lesson:** A happy post can be true without showing the whole day. Comparing a full
life with someone else's selected slice creates a distorted scorecard.

**Scenario:** A fictional peer posts a genuinely joyful fishing photo with the true
caption `I CAUGHT THIS ONE!`. The tight crop and forced perspective make the catch
look enormous and solitary. The corrupted site—not necessarily the peer—adds
`LEGENDARY FISH`, `SOLO WILDERNESS MASTER`, and an `AVOCADO%` comparison score. As
the frame expands, Finn sees helpers, a stocked barrel or tank, many similar fish,
tangled gear, cleanup, and a busier setting. The joy remains real; the inferred life
story does not.

### Passage and visual flow

| # | Act | Reading purpose | Site change |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 | The peer's upbeat but truthful caption | Removes one AI-added superlative. |
| 2 | 1 | A friend's account of helping with the catch | Widens the crop enough to reveal a helper's hand. |
| 3 | 1 | A photo caption about forced perspective | Restores the ordinary scale of the fish. |
| 4 | 1 | Short narrative about tangled gear and cleanup | Extends the timeline beyond the trophy moment. |
| 5 | 1 | Two viewpoints on the same good day | Reveals the full scene and clears Act 1. |
| 6 | 2 | Observation-versus-inference passage | Locks `POSTED MOMENT`. |
| 7 | 2 | Selected-slice and comparison passage | Locks `NOT A WHOLE-LIFE SCORE`. |
| 8 | 2 | Synthesis preserving both joy and context | Locks `KEEP THE WIDER FRAME` and secures the feed. |

**Midpoint:** Chinmay: “I told the AI to enhance the posts and make all the posts
awesome.” The fixed-size photo frame snaps back into increasingly impossible trophy
crops and the Honesty Meter ricochets from `12%` to `AVOCADO%` to `BANANA%`. Amy:
“The happy moment was already real. The AI turned one selected picture into a whole
story about somebody's life.”

**Act 2 anchors:** separate the posted moment from added inference; remove whole-life
comparison; retain wider context.

**Secured ending:** The genuine catch remains inside the same-size hero frame, now
zoomed out to show the wider scene and no social-ranking claim.

**Reflection prompt:** “What lesson did we learn that we should teach the AI? Explain
what about the fishing post was true, what the crop left out, and why comparisons
with selected posts can be misleading.”

**Post-reflection close:** Chinmay: “I get it now. Real life does not need to be
enhanced to make it seem more awesome.” AI receipt: `LEARNED: A CROP IS A CHOICE,
NOT A WHOLE LIFE`.

**Anchor components:** one fixed-size image viewport with pre-authored zoom levels,
AI inference badges, expanding timeline strip, and a small Honesty Meter displaying
nonsensical percentages. The meter replaces the standard Corruption Meter only on
this site and must remain a supporting joke, not the visual centerpiece.

## 4. MyCorner — Amy Is Definitely Abroad

**Lesson:** Images and profile details are easy to copy. Before sending money or
trusting an urgent request, verify identity through information or contact routes the
request itself does not control.

**Scenario:** A terrible impersonator holds a printed picture of Amy on a popsicle
stick in front of their face. The profile claims, “Hello, it is me, Amy. I am stranded
abroad after the Royal USB Conference. Send money now and I will return ten times as
much.” The scenario parodies the classic advance-fee scam, but the central lesson is
identity and impersonation. The game contains no working payment control, bank field,
external link, or real transaction.

### Passage and visual flow

| # | Act | Reading purpose | Site change |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 | Amy's known profile biography | Restores her established profile details beside the copy. |
| 2 | 1 | New-account history | Reveals that the impersonator profile appeared minutes ago. |
| 3 | 1 | Absurd stranded-abroad message | Highlights urgency and the promised return. |
| 4 | 1 | Timeline and location contradiction | Reveals mutually impossible posts. |
| 5 | 1 | Known-route verification dialogue | Restores a separate way to check with Amy and clears Act 1. |
| 6 | 2 | Image-versus-identity passage | Locks `A PHOTO IS NOT A PERSON`. |
| 7 | 2 | Account-history comparison | Locks `CHECK THE TIMELINE`. |
| 8 | 2 | Verification procedure | Locks `VERIFY ANOTHER WAY`. |
| 9 | 2 | Impersonation and money-request recap | Locks `DO NOT SEND` and secures the profile. |

**Midpoint:** Chinmay: “The original profile was low quality, so I asked the AI to
enhance it.” AI status: `UPSCALING IDENTITY TO 4K / GENERATING BACKGROUND / ADDING
VERIFICATION PICTURES`. The paper mask becomes glossy, the profile receives an
AI-generated royal airport background and seventeen fake verification images, and
the request promises an even larger return. Amy: “That is still my picture on a
stick. Better image quality does not verify a person.”

**Act 2 anchors:** photo is not identity; check history/timeline; verify outside the
request; do not send money.

**Secured ending:** The real Amy profile remains and the impersonator is labeled
`IDENTITY NOT VERIFIED`; the ridiculous mask is plainly visible.

**Reflection prompt:** “What lesson did we learn that we should teach the AI? Explain
which clues showed that the profile did not prove Amy's identity and what the AI
should recommend when an online profile urgently asks for money.”

**Post-reflection close:** Amy: “Anyone can be fooled by a convincing copy. Pausing
gives you another way to check.” Chinmay: “I see it now. A sharper picture only made
the impersonation sharper.” AI receipt: `LEARNED: VERIFY THE PERSON, NOT THE PICTURE`.

**Anchor components:** authentic and impersonator profiles, paper-mask image,
message popup, account-age/timeline strip, location contradictions, verification
route, Corruption Meter, and four Act 2 locks.

## 5. Yahuh Portal — The Moon Has Resigned

**Lesson:** A sensational headline is designed to sell attention, not necessarily to
summarize a story reliably. Read the article, support, context, and latest update.

**Scenario:** The portal screams absurd fictional world events such as `MOON RESIGNS
FROM NIGHT SHIFT`, `OCEAN NOW 40% SOUP`, and `PIGEONS DEMAND MAYORAL RECOUNT`.
Exact events remain copy-level choices. The important structure is a headline, image,
article body, quoted source, update/correction, and clearly labeled promotion.

### Passage and visual flow

| # | Act | Reading purpose | Site change |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 | Headline paired with its actual opening paragraph | Restores the story body beneath one headline. |
| 2 | 1 | Image caption and wider context | Shrinks a misleading alarm-thumbnail treatment. |
| 3 | 1 | Full quotation behind a chopped quote | Restores omitted words. |
| 4 | 1 | Publication timeline | Restores the update timestamp. |
| 5 | 1 | Correction note and promotional label | Separates news, update, and ad; clears Act 1. |
| 6 | 2 | Headline-versus-body comparison | Locks `READ THE STORY`. |
| 7 | 2 | Claim and support comparison | Locks `SHOW WHAT SUPPORTS IT`. |
| 8 | 2 | Quote and context comparison | Locks `KEEP THE CONTEXT`. |
| 9 | 2 | Update/correction synthesis | Locks `SHOW WHAT CHANGED` and secures the portal. |

**Midpoint:** Chinmay: “I solved news. I told the AI to make every headline so clear
that nobody needs the article.” AI status: `MAXIMIZING CLICKS PER EXCLAMATION MARK`.
The portal becomes a wall of flashing emergencies; one advertisement disguises
itself as breaking news. Amy: “Headlines need to match the story, or people walk away
with the wrong idea. Let’s restore what each article actually says.”

**Act 2 anchors:** read the body; show support; keep quote/image context; retain
updates, corrections, and promotion labels.

**Secured ending:** The sensational cards become accurate but still playful:
`NEW MOON NEXT WEEK`, `SHIP CARRYING SOUP SAILS ACROSS THE PACIFIC`, and `PIGEON
MASCOT WINS SCHOOL FUNDRAISER VOTE`. Each sits beside an accurate summary, support,
and current update.

**Reflection prompt:** “What lesson did we learn that we should teach the AI? Explain
how the headlines changed the stories and what the AI must preserve when it makes
news more attention-grabbing.”

**Post-reflection close:** Chinmay: “I see the problem. If the headline changes the
story, it is not helping anyone understand the news.” AI receipt: `LEARNED: HEADLINES
MUST MATCH THE STORY`.

**Anchor components:** three news cards, headline/body pairs, image captions, quote
panel, timestamp/correction strip, promotion label, Corruption Meter, and four Act 2
locks.

describes a platform incentive rather than Finn's interest. The site is funny because

`OPTIMIZING FOR THE MOST ADS / AUTOPLAYING PARTS 1–47 / SEARCH INTENT REMOVED`.
The queue refills with `MOST ADS`, `PART 1 OF 83`, and `AN AD INSIDE AN AD`. Amy:
“Those are goals for the site, not goals Finn chose. Let’s give him search and the

**Secured ending:** Finn finds the video matching his actual hobby and deliberately

`LEARNED: USER CHOICE IS IMPORTANT`.

**Anchor components:** player shell, broken/restored search, queue cards, autoplay
toggle, ad/runtime badges, `Suggested`/`Searched for` labels, Corruption Meter, and
four Act 2 locks.

## 7. Search-ish — The Search Page That Hid the Search

**Lesson:** AI answers and ads can be useful, but they must not crowd out the results
that answer Finn's actual request. The page should clearly distinguish an optional AI
answer, a paid placement, and ordinary results—and leave Finn in control of where he
goes next.

**Scenario:** Finn searches `GET A COPY OF ADVENTURES OF HUCKLEBERRY FINN`. A huge AI
overview takes over the top of the page and announces `YOU DON'T NEED THE BOOK. I
READ IT. JUST ASK ME.` Beneath it, an almost-unlabeled sponsored result from `INTERNET
MEGA BOOKSTORE` claims it is the only place to buy books and promises delivery in
five seconds. The two results that most directly answer Finn's request—a library copy
and a local bookstore copy—are tiny, muted, and pushed to the bottom.

Project Gutenberg eBook 76 provides a public-domain-in-the-USA full text suitable for
candidate passage selection. Exact excerpts remain a content-review decision because
the novel's treatment of slavery, racism, and historical language requires context;
public-domain status does not remove that responsibility.

### Passage and visual flow

| # | Act | Reading purpose | Site change |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 | Search query and book/catalog record | Restores Finn's exact request and the correct title/author. |
| 2 | 1 | Short passage about what an AI overview is and is not | Shrinks the overview enough to reveal the result list. |
| 3 | 1 | Paid-placement label and claim comparison | Enlarges the `SPONSORED` label and removes `ONLY PLACE TO BUY BOOKS`. |
| 4 | 1 | Library catalog entry and availability note | Restores the library result as a normal, readable result. |
| 5 | 1 | Local bookstore listing and pickup information | Restores the local-store result and clears Act 1. |
| 6 | 2 | AI answer versus Finn's actual search goal | Locks `ASK AI IS OPTIONAL`. |
| 7 | 2 | Ad-label and extravagant-shipping-claim check | Locks `LABEL THE AD`. |
| 8 | 2 | Library and bookstore result-fit comparison | Locks `SHOW USEFUL RESULTS`. |
| 9 | 2 | Final result-order rationale | Locks `LET FINN CHOOSE` and secures search. |

**Midpoint:** Chinmay: “I made the search much faster. I told the AI that if it has
already read the book, Finn does not need the rest of the page.” AI status:
`AI ANSWER EXPANDED / SPONSORED RESULT BOOSTED / ORDINARY RESULTS MINIMIZED`. The AI
overview and Mega Bookstore ad inflate until the library and local store nearly
disappear. Amy: “Finn searched for a copy of the book. The AI answer and the ad should
not hide the places that actually have one. Let's put each kind of result back in its
proper place.”

**Act 2 anchors:** make AI help optional and compact; label the paid result clearly;
rank the library and local-bookstore results where Finn can see them; preserve Finn's
choice of where to get the book.

**Secured ending:** The library and local bookstore are prominent results with clear
availability information. The Mega Bookstore remains visible but unmistakably labeled
`SPONSORED`. A compact `ASK AI` option sits below the main results instead of replacing
them.

**Reflection prompt:** “What lesson did we learn that we should teach the AI? Explain
why an AI answer or ad should not hide the results Finn searched for, and what the
page should let Finn choose.”

**Post-reflection close:** Chinmay: “Oh. Making one answer enormous did not make the
search better. It just made the actual results harder to find.” AI receipt:
`LEARNED: LABEL ADS, SHOW USEFUL RESULTS, LET THE USER CHOOSE`.

**Anchor components:** search box, oversized/compact AI overview, Corruption Meter,
barely visible/clear `SPONSORED` label, Mega Bookstore card, library result, local
bookstore result, result-ranking layout, and four Act 2 locks.

**Content note:** Public-domain excerpts from a verified edition may still appear in
the Reading Companion, but the mission no longer depends on a literary-interpretation
question. Any excerpt containing sensitive historical language still requires
educator/content review and context.

## 8. Amaze-On — Sponsored Soccer Shoe Avalanche

**Lesson:** Sponsored ranking, unreliable review signals, urgency, and shipping speed
can crowd out fit, durability, informed choice, and avoidable waste.

expensive sponsored branded shoe endorsed by superstar `Captain Goalazo`, and the
absolute cheapest shoe with poor durability, vague environmental claims, excessive
packaging, and rush delivery. Fit information and meaningful review context are
buried. This is a fictional corruption, not a claim that a real retailer creates or
rewards fake reviews.

| 4 | 1 | Mixed review set with dates and reviewer histories | Restores critical reviews and review context. |
| 5 | 1 | Durability/repair comparison | Reveals expected use and replacement risk. |
| 6 | 1 | Delivery and packaging comparison | Restores slower consolidated delivery and clears Act 1. |
| 7 | 2 | Ranking and sponsorship comparison | Locks `SHOW WHY IT RANKS`. |
| 8 | 2 | Review-source and pattern check | Locks `SHOW REVIEW CONTEXT`. |
| 9 | 2 | Fit and durability decision | Locks `MATCH THE ACTUAL NEED`. |
| 10 | 2 | Shipping/package/waste comparison | Locks `DELIVERY IS A CHOICE` and secures the page. |

**Midpoint:** Chinmay: “I helped teach the AI to guess what people want so they do

and the disposable bargain shoe until they cover the fit guide; five-star bursts and

**Act 2 anchors:** disclose ranking/sponsorship; restore review context rather than

**Post-reflection close:** Chinmay: “Oh, I guess there are a lot of benefits to
letting customers make their own choices about what works best for them.” AI receipt:
`LEARNED: THE CUSTOMER NEEDS REAL CHOICES AND USEFUL DETAILS`.

**Anchor components:** search/filter bar, two-result product-detail comparison,
sponsor badge, specs/fit panel, mixed review panel, durability note, delivery/package
selector, Corruption Meter, and four locks.

**Content gate:** Final review lessons should follow current consumer-protection
guidance: suspicious patterns are clues, not proof that Finn can identify every fake
review by sight.

## 9. Spotty-Fi — The Song With No People In It

**Lesson:** Music discovery works better when artists and all credited contributors
remain visible. Generated abundance should not erase attribution or the path to learn
about and follow creators.

**Scenario:** A persistent player and a compact horizontal creator strip show the
current song and its people. The center of the screen is

discovery surface fills with anonymous AI-generated tracks and album art while
deleting artist names, contributor roles, About information, credits, follow
controls, and related works. This site is about discoverability and attribution, not
ads and not one displaced local artist.

The attached reference supports a useful information hierarchy, not a layout to
copy: persistent player, center-stage discovery, and accessible `About the artist`
and multi-role `Credits`. The production direction transforms those ideas into
three discovery cards and one horizontal creator strip so the site remains legible
inside the game's two-thirds-width website window.

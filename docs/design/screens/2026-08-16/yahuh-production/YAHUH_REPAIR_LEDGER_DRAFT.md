# Yahuh production repair ledger v3

Status: production review candidate, not a design freeze. Exact dialogue and
microcopy remain editable.

## Directed lesson

A headline should accurately summarize a story, not replace it. Readers need
the story, its supporting sources, the image caption, and the author to
understand what actually happened.

## Fixed page grammar

- Purple Yahuh portal header with search, navigation, mail, and weather.
- One dominant moon story and two compact stories: a soup shipment and a
  school pigeon-mascot vote.
- Every story uses the same native reporting modules: story, sources, image
  caption, and author.
- The headline begins as the dominant shape in each article. In the lead story
  it forms a backward L around the only visible picture; in the compact stories
  it covers nearly the entire article.
- The article composition deliberately reflows as reporting returns. Headlines
  contract non-linearly and the story, source, caption, and author claim the
  space they uncover. The outer site cards and shared desktop stay fixed.
- Headline type is re-centered and resized inside every new headline shape; the
  container never shrinks while leaving its wording stranded against an edge.
- The reporting hierarchy is story first, image caption second, then compact
  source and author metadata. Source boxes never outweigh the story box.
- Every reporting row uses the same native grammar: a green `STORY:`,
  `CAPTION:`, `SOURCE:`, or `BY:` label followed by neutral-black content.
  Reporting copy is left-aligned like a real article. Each label-and-copy block
  is vertically centered as one unit; it is never horizontally centered merely
  because its container is wide.
- Type follows the reporting hierarchy and the available geometry rather than
  one global size. Story copy is largest, captions are secondary, and source
  and byline metadata are smaller. Lead-story type may be larger than compact
  sidebar type, but every row remains legible and uses its box deliberately.
- The lead story uses one full-width reporting stack through the author reveal.
  Once the accurate picture returns, the picture, headline, and story share the
  top grid while caption, source, and byline remain full-width rows below.
- Compact stories distribute their revealed rows evenly through the full card
  height. Adding a row recomposes the complete card; it never leaves a stranded
  metadata box or an unexplained empty band at the bottom.
- Red wording remains identical until its repair. Once repaired, the wording
  and its green state remain fixed through the rest of the run.
- The shared desktop and Reading Companion geometry do not move.

## First repair run — six readings

1. **Reveal the stories.** The oversized headlines shrink enough for the story
   text to emerge underneath.
2. **Restore the sources.** Each story regains a specific supporting source.
3. **Restore image captions.** The pictures gain context without changing yet.
4. **Restore the authors.** Each story receives a visible byline.
5. **Make the pictures match.** The sensational pictures become an ordinary
   moon calendar, a soup-cargo ship on blue water, and a student in a pigeon
   costume. The inaccurate headlines remain red.
6. **Make the headlines match.** The headlines finally become:
   - `NEW MOON NEXT WEEK`
   - `SHIP CARRYING SOUP SAILS ACROSS PACIFIC`
   - `PIGEON MASCOT WINS SCHOOL FUNDRAISER VOTE`

## Auto over-fix

Chinmay tells Auto to put the news in the headline. Auto decides that headlines
should contain 100 percent of the news, expands them to fill every card, and
removes the pictures and reporting underneath. The lead story is taken over by
the canonical toaster-like Auto, still Bluetooth enabled, cheerfully aiming a
megaphone at the headline.

## Lock-in run — three readings

This run is deliberately shorter than the first run.

1. **BRING BACK THE PICTURES.** The accurate pictures return while the
   reporting remains removed and the overblown headlines stay red.
2. **RESTORE THE STORIES.** Story text, sources, captions, and authors return
   together. The headlines are still wrong.
3. **FIX THE HEADLINES.** The accurate headlines return and the secured page
   exactly matches the repaired first-run bookend.

## Review sequence

The click-through reviewer contains thirteen states:

1. Initial corruption — headline covers the reporting
2. Story emerges
3. Sources return
4. Image captions return
5. Authors return
6. Pictures match the stories
7. Headlines match the stories
8. Auto headline takeover
9. Green lock checklist over the same takeover
10. Pictures restored and first lock secured
11. Reporting restored and second lock secured
12. Headlines fixed and all locks secured
13. Secured page with the checklist closed

# Amaze-On Production Contract

Status: full site-state working candidate for Chinmay review; not a final visual or storyline freeze.

## Preserved direction

- Four fixed product-card slots in a 2 × 2 grid across every state. The grid
  leaves enough room for review, fit, durability, delivery, and ranking context.
- Initial corruption repeats the paid Captain Goalazo placement three times and
  places the paid absolute-cheapest placement fourth. Both extremes are
  sponsored; low price itself is not framed as the problem.
- The repaired state restores four distinct choices, meaningful filters, mixed review context, fit/durability/delivery information, clear paid-placement labeling, and `Cart (0)`.
- No repaired result is selected for Finn.
- Canonical corruption red is reserved for hidden, distorted, locked, generated, or unresolved interface content. Truthfully disclosed negative product facts use neutral storefront colors.
- `SHOPPING CONTROL` is the site-specific progress label.

The repaired ranking makes the algorithmic change visible rather than merely
changing labels: `Best Match` and `Great Value` occupy the top row; the flimsy
cheap shoe and Goalazzo shoe move to the lower row as conspicuous paid ads with
their fit, review, durability, material, delivery, and waste details exposed.

## Auto over-fix candidate

Chinmay's incomplete direction to guess what Finn wants is interpreted by Auto
as permission to choose, add, and ship products. The over-fix keeps four product
slots with three paid Goalazzo placements and one paid flimsy shoe, changes the
search field to `AUTO ALREADY CHOSE FOR FINN`, raises the cart to four, locks
changes, and displays a prominent Auto-cart confirmation with quantities, total,
shipping status, and skipped human confirmation.

This is a fictional comic escalation. The eventual repair sequence must restore sponsorship context, useful reviews, fit/durability information, delivery and waste choices, human confirmation, and `Cart (0)`.

## State ledger

The first repair run uses six passages and orders the changes from smallest to
largest so the storefront still reads as corrupted until the final reveal:

1. Restore Finn's size.
2. Restore brand and spending filters.
3. Restore mixed reviews, dates, and reviewer history.
4. Restore grouped-delivery information and its fewer-trips/fewer-boxes tradeoff.
5. Restore fit, durability, and replacement-waste details inside the existing cards.
6. Perform the dominant re-ranking: useful `Best Match` and `Great Value` choices appear, while the two distorted extremes remain available as clearly disclosed paid ads.

The Auto over-fix then returns the distorted four-card set, adds all four items
to the cart, begins shipping, and says Finn's confirmation was skipped for
efficiency. The lock-in replay uses five player-facing repairs:

1. `SHOW PAID PLACEMENT`
2. `SHOW REAL REVIEWS`
3. `SHOW ALL CHOICES`
4. `SHOW DELIVERY + WASTE`
5. `ASK BEFORE BUYING`

The checklist is a green repair container with unresolved items in canonical
red. Each completed item turns green and produces its matching storefront
change. The fifth lock restores the useful ranking and `Cart (0)`; the secured
state removes the checklist. No player-facing screen uses internal act or phase
labels.

The initial paid-placement labels deliberately escalate the joke: `#1 · PAID
PLACEMENT`, `#2 · ALSO PAID PLACEMENT`, and `#3 · YOU GUESSED IT — PAID AGAIN`.
The fourth cheap placement is also explicitly paid so the lesson cannot be read
as "budget is bad."

## Current refinement recommendation

The strongest lesson is not that expensive or cheap products are inherently bad. It is that paid rank, price extremes, stars, and speed cannot substitute for the information Finn needs—or for Finn's permission. The auto-buy escalation makes loss of agency the midpoint payoff while the storefront evidence carries the supporting waste and false-advertising lesson.

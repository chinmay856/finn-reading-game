# Internet Recovery 98 — simulated persona playthrough review

Status: **heuristic review of the August 26 production build; not feedback from recruited players**

This review applies the previously documented skeptical, casual, creative/social,
and cautious teen lenses (ages 14–15), plus the cautious/traditional and
progressive/media-literacy parent lenses. It is a design critique technique, not
a substitute for the upcoming external playtest.

## What was reviewed

- The complete ten-site campaign at 1440 × 900, including all 92 passage-driven
  visual transitions, midpoint over-fixes, repair locks, reflections, receipts,
  resumable replays, and new-game behavior.
- Shared screens: player login, local-model dial-up, Recovery Browser, Reading
  Companion, reading result, vocabulary audio, Documents, Start menu, and
  desktop chrome.
- The integrated endgame: desktop takeover, six sequential Auto pop-ups,
  ten three-part document repairs, final instruction, Techno cascade, epilogue,
  saved lessons, and replay destinations.
- Automated checks accompanied the visual pass: correct frame order, no broken
  images or failed page assets, no game-stage overflow, and no renderer errors.

## Shared-screen feedback

| Screen or mechanic | Teen lenses | Parent lenses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Login and dial-up | The retro joke gives model loading a purpose. Hiding prior player names avoids a public-looking profile list. | Clear that saves are local to the device; no voice recording is presented as being uploaded. |
| Recovery Browser | Ten sites fit without scrolling and the recovered/corrupted states are distinguishable. The grid is dense, but the varied site art makes choosing manageable. | Progress and replay state are understandable without displaying the player's name. |
| Reading Companion | Source attribution is short, the current passage has natural sentence-level chunks, and retry is voluntary. Some nineteenth-century prose will still be the hardest part of the game. | Comprehension remains separate from speech scoring, a low score never blocks progress, and vocabulary support is available without exposing a definition wall. |
| Repair locks | The immediate visual change makes reading feel consequential. Repeated checkbox language risks feeling formulaic, but each site's art and joke keep the sequence legible. | Repairs consistently restore evidence, consent, context, authorship, or choice. Red/green states also use labels and icons. |
| Reflection and receipt | A live word count silently suggests detail without enforcing it. Seeing Auto restate the lesson makes the writing feel used. | The open response is not graded, and the saved reflection becomes meaningful in the endgame rather than becoming disposable data. |
| Techno | Reactions are expressive and usually settle while reading. The final cascade is delightfully excessive. | Motion has a reduced-motion path and can be stopped immediately. |
| Diagnostics | Keeping shortcuts above the authored stage makes the playtest fast without putting prototype language in the game. | Diagnostics should remain visibly separate from the child-facing interface in shared tests. |

## Site-by-site feedback

| Site | 14-year-old perspective | Parent perspectives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WikiWhy | The confidence-versus-evidence joke is clear, and the source/history repairs are visible. Huxley and Bacon make this the most demanding opening; test whether a first-time player wants a lighter first choice. | Strong media-literacy lesson: clarity does not erase sources, edit history, disagreement, or honest uncertainty. |
| ViewTube | One of the strongest and funniest arcs. Search intent, excessive ads, autoplay, and the final viewer choice are concrete rather than preachy. | Preserves teen agency while making recommendation pressure visible; it does not declare entertainment itself harmful. |
| FacePlace | The praise-only over-fix is visually obvious and the ordinary-life restoration is sympathetic. Real teens should confirm the dialogue does not sound adult-written. | Critiques feed incentives without calling positive posts dishonest or asking the player to diagnose a friend's private life. |
| ThreadIt | Copied replies and identical opinions make the problem obvious. The repeated-post visuals are busy, but the repair verbs explain what is changing. | Values independent sourcing and legitimate disagreement without treating consensus as automatically false. |
| Yahuh! Portal | The moon/news collage is memorable and silly. It is one of the busier compositions, so the large repair labels do important work. | Clear separation of reporting, sources, headlines, and non-news material; avoids a political definition of “balanced.” |
| MapGuess | The moving library and unchanged route are the clearest visual contradiction in the campaign. Repeated failed locks read as a joke after the first red X, but should be watched in live playtesting in case someone assumes a bug. | Teaches that a system should not move the goal to preserve a confident answer. Failure is nonpunitive and visibly recoverable. |
| Amaze-On | Automatic buying is instantly understandable, and the ridiculous shoes make the over-fix funny. The final “ask before buying” action is concrete. | Strong consent and consumer-choice framing; no real purchase occurs, and paid placement, reviews, delivery, and waste remain visible. |
| Search-ish | The unwanted summary and sponsored shortcut are clear. “Keep the search” is slightly more abstract than the other repair verbs, so casual players may need the final visual hierarchy to understand the win. | Good distinction among optional AI help, advertising, libraries, free editions, and real search results. |
| Spotty-Fi | Artist identity and listener choice are appealing, but the dark interface plus credits, queue, volume, and discovery make this the most cognitively dense site. | Strong creator-rights and autonomy lesson. It suggests rather than chooses and keeps attribution visible without asking the player to detect AI by sound. |
| MyCorner | The impostor/money escalation creates real stakes, and “pause before ever sending money” is a usable rule. The verification steps feel protective rather than punitive. | Strongest safety case: known-route verification, account history, another channel, and a pause before money. The copy avoids blaming the person targeted. |

## Endgame feedback

- The one-second, one-at-a-time Auto pop-ups create a convincing desktop takeover.
  Their large, irregular overlap and last-in-first-out closing order explain the
  interaction without an on-screen counter.
- The “go outside and touch grass” joke lands because it is a plausible Auto
  over-interpretation rather than an evil threat.
- The Instruction Builder successfully turns the ten saved reflections into a
  payoff: restore Auto's lesson, restore the player's explanation, then add the
  missing boundary. The explicit **Move on to next site** beat lets the player
  see all three green checks.
- Thirty selections are intentionally substantial. The skeptical and casual
  teen lenses flag repetition/fatigue as the largest endgame risk; real-player
  timing should determine whether later documents need faster pacing.
- The persistent, varied Techno trails now read like the old Solitaire ending.
  The center prompt, click-to-stop behavior, cleanup, and reduced-motion path
  keep the celebration controllable.

## Priorities for the external playtest

1. Watch whether WikiWhy's opening prose feels challenging or discouraging.
2. Time the ten-document Instruction Builder and note where attention drops.
3. Ask whether Spotty-Fi has one clear primary lesson despite its denser interface.
4. Confirm MapGuess's repeated failed repair reads as intentional comedy.
5. Ask both parent lenses whether MyCorner feels reassuring and non-blaming.
6. Include one motion-sensitive player in the Techno-cascade review.

## Readiness judgment

No blocking visual, asset, progression, replay, or stability issue was found in
this pass. The build is ready for external playtesting. The questions above are
research targets, not reasons to delay sharing, and this document should not be
quoted as real teen or parent approval.

## Material decisions for the next review

### 1. What exactly is WikiWhy parodying?

The current concept is unmistakably an encyclopedia: globe cue, article column,
citations, edit history, and Wikipedia-like research behavior. In conversation,
“wikiHow” was also mentioned, which is an instructional how-to product with a
different visual identity and lesson.

**Recommendation:** confirm WikiWhy as a fictional collaborative encyclopedia,
not a how-to site. If both are desired, make them separate concepts rather than
blending their identities.

### 2. Is FacePlace primarily about interpersonal inference or echo chambers?

The revised plot focuses on Finn's temptation to infer someone's whole life or
feelings from a real but curated happy post. Echo chambers concern repeated
beliefs and social agreement, which would overlap more with ThreadIt and Yahuh's
information-selection lesson.

**Recommendation:** keep FacePlace about social comparison and responding without
assumption. Let ThreadIt own apparent consensus and Yahuh own personalized news
coverage blind spots.

### 3. Is Spotty-Fi primarily about impersonation/consent or economic displacement?

The current story teaches a concrete, supportable behavior: do not infer by sound;
check the artist's information, distinguish disclosed assistance from an artist-
reported unauthorized use, and route uncertainty for review. A story about jobs,
royalties, or “original human art versus generated music” would require a broader,
more politically and legally complex plot.

**Recommendation:** approve creator consent and honest attribution as the primary
lesson. Treat compensation and industry displacement as optional context, not the
required puzzle.

### 4. How broad should Amaze-On's ethical lesson be?

The revised plot can responsibly show fit, budget, total cost, durability,
repair/reuse, packaging, and only verifiable claims. It cannot honestly assign a
simple labor or environmental morality score to fictional products.

**Recommendation:** approve informed choice and full useful-life fit as the main
lesson. Let waste be a visible consequence and reserve labor/supply-chain ethics
for optional reading or a separately researched story.

### 5. Does ViewTube's attention/autoplay story replace the old duplicate-video story?

Making duplication the main puzzle caused ViewTube to overlap ThreadIt and
Search-ish. The revised version makes deliberate attention and `Selected by you`
the payoff; misleading reaction copies are background comedy.

**Recommendation:** approve attention autonomy as ViewTube's single lesson.

### 6. What causes the ten corruptions?

All ten local plots currently work without deciding whether Chinmay knowingly
causes them, sincerely deploys an unsafe AI, or tries to contain an autonomous
system. Character dialogue and campaign escalation cannot be finalized until this
is decided once.

**Recommendation:** Chinmay is sincerely reckless and increasingly alarmed; his
AI becomes the antagonist by continuing to optimize narrow goals after he tries
to stop it. He is responsible but not secretly malicious.

### 7. May the real platform names appear anywhere player-facing?

The internal design research uses real services to ground features and risks. The
fictional game can be recognizable without naming the real companies in the UI.

**Recommendation:** use real names only in internal research/credits/legal review,
not in player-facing story or site copy. Use minimal transformed cues and obtain
qualified intellectual-property review before public release.

### 8. May the visual team adopt the fixed shell now?

The current candidate is 1440 × 900, roughly 68% site canvas and 32% fixed right
Reading Companion, with proportional scaling or letterboxing. The site is layered
art; only the Reading Companion and one approved site action need real controls.

**Recommendation:** approve this as the first storyboard master. Other aspect
ratios, if needed, receive separate approved compositions rather than free-flowing
responsive rearrangement.

### 9. Is Finn a visible named protagonist or the player directly?

The historical story premise names Finn, and the candidate plots currently use
that name for clarity. This role has not been re-approved in the current review
cycle. The choice affects dialogue, character art, personalization, and whether
the player watches or inhabits the protagonist.

**Recommendation:** keep Finn as the named player character in dialogue and story
records, but do not show a fixed portrait by default. Let the player inhabit Finn's
role unless a later character-board review finds a visible protagonist valuable.

## Decisions already inferred and applied

These follow directly from Chinmay's direction or convergent reviews and do not
need separate approval unless they feel wrong during story review:

- Character sheets are accepted working directions, not frozen runtime assets.
- Each site has one main lesson, one reading clue, one bounded decision, and one
  visible consequence.
- Amy waits for Finn's hypothesis; she does not announce the solution first.
- Techno acts rarely, settles during highlighted reading, and never supplies a
  required answer without evidence established in the passage.
- Wrong choices preserve progress and reveal more evidence.
- `Unknown`, `Not enough evidence`, `Do nothing yet`, and `Ask for help` can be
  successful outcomes.
- Fictional purchases, messages, accounts, and routes have no real consequence.
- Secondary lessons appear as optional details or secured-state controls, not
  additional required puzzles.
- Player-facing language is ordinary; research vocabulary remains in internal
  documentation.
- The game critiques a feature or incentive and still shows why the service is
  useful. It does not declare a platform, its users, or technology generally evil.

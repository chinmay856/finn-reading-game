# Multi-persona plot review — synthesis

Status: **historical scenario review; later live-review directions supersede its
MyCorner and Yahuh plots; not real user research**

The first ten-site plot draft was independently reviewed through four teen and
two parent perspectives. These are deliberately varied agent personas, not
interviews with real people and not substitutes for later playtesting.

## Review panel

| Persona | Primary lens |
| --- | --- |
| Skeptical teen, 14 | Detects fake choices, adult-written dialogue, and lessons that underestimate teens. |
| Creative/social teen, 15 | Emotional stakes, character appeal, music/art nuance, and desire to play. |
| Casual gamer, 14 | Ten-second comprehension, pacing, large visual verbs, and tutorial overload. |
| Cautious teen, 14 | Anxiety, recovery, nonpunitive failure, privacy, money, and physical safety. |
| Cautious/traditional parent | Practical habits, family escalation, ideological neutrality, and anti-business overreach. |
| Progressive/media-literacy parent | Teen autonomy, inclusion, structural incentives, creator rights, and victim-blame risks. |

## Strong convergence

All or nearly all perspectives agreed that:

- MapGuess has the clearest visual contradiction and strongest pilot potential.
- MyCorner teaches a valuable, concrete verification habit but needs gentle,
  non-victim-blaming safety framing.
- Amaze-On has strong agency and comedy but must respect budget constraints and
  make clear that no real purchase occurs.
- Yahuh was overloaded and “build a balanced front page” sounded subjective and
  adult-assigned.
- Spotty-Fi was responsible but too dense; it tried to teach consent, copyright,
  attribution, compensation, impersonation, and AI ethics at once.
- ThreadIt and Search-ish were too similar because both collapsed many items into
  one origin.
- ViewTube should own attention/autoplay control rather than become another
  copied-source investigation.
- FacePlace should reveal what a feed leaves outside the frame without implying
  happy posts are dishonest or inviting Finn to diagnose a friend's private life.
- Important actions need ordinary language, visible control, saved progress, and
  no punishment for a plausible wrong choice.

## Important perspective differences

- The skeptical and casual teens preferred immediate physical jokes and fewer
  named frameworks; the cautious teen was comfortable with evidence-heavy
  WikiWhy and Search-ish when recovery remained clear.
- The cautious parent wanted official or trusted-adult escalation for money,
  impersonation, physical safety, and school notices. The progressive parent
  agreed on support paths but warned against making adult obedience the win state.
- The cautious parent wanted clear, checkable purchase specifications and neutral
  business framing. The progressive parent additionally wanted budget, used,
  borrowed, repair, labor, and environmental constraints acknowledged without a
  magic ethics score.
- The progressive parent wanted more structural accountability; the cautious
  parent wanted personal responsibility emphasized. The revision keeps both:
  Finn gains a usable protective action, while the secured state also changes the
  system rule or transparency control that created the pressure.

## Revisions applied to the candidate plots

- Added persistent `Pause`, `Back`, and `Try again`; wrong choices reveal evidence
  and never erase reading work.
- Made `Unknown`, `Not enough evidence`, `Do nothing yet`, and `Ask for help`
  valid outcomes where appropriate.
- WikiWhy now uses a concrete dog-color claim and permits `CONTESTED — KEEP
  CONTEXT` rather than always locking truth.
- ThreadIt now centers a school-event rumor, three readable branches, one low-vote
  independent update, and pausing before sharing.
- FacePlace now uncrops an ordinary day, asks Finn to choose a socially plausible
  response, and critiques feed selection—not the friend's choice to post happily.
- MyCorner now uses a compatible pause → known-route verification → block/report
  sequence and confirms nothing was sent or accessed.
- Yahuh now gives Finn and Amy a concrete bus deadline, asks whether to trust
  either personalized page or widen beyond both, and adds a broader-view lane
  instead of constructing a “balanced” page.
- ViewTube now begins with Finn's viewing intention and centers pausing and
  deliberately choosing what plays next.
- Search-ish now evaluates which source fits a concrete foam-board glue question;
  only ThreadIt retains the many-copies/one-origin mechanic.
- Amaze-On now uses a visibly fictional tripod comparison, explicit budget, three
  criteria, borrow/used/buy-nothing options, and zero-charge confirmation. The
  midpoint reranks a poor fit instead of introducing a second subscription plot.
- Spotty-Fi now centers one artist-supplied unauthorized impersonation claim;
  Finn checks official information and routes the claim for review rather than
  detecting AI by sound or adjudicating consent.
- MapGuess is explicitly pre-trip planning with a concrete library deadline; the
  impossible lake route is primary, with sponsored placement as a labeled
  secondary temptation attributable to the fictional corruption.

## Still requiring real-player validation

- Whether a teen understands each lesson after play without seeing its design
  documentation.
- Whether the reading clue feels like gameplay instead of a quiz.
- Whether the corrupted state is legible within ten seconds.
- Whether the wrong choices feel plausible and the recovery feels fair.
- Whether any sensitive plot triggers real anxiety despite the practice framing.
- Whether humor, character likeness, motion, and copy work in the actual visual
  composition.

The next milestone after Chinmay approves the story contracts is a storyboard
test—not implementation—followed by real teen/parent playtesting when practical.

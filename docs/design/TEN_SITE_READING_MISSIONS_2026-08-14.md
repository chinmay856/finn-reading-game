# Ten reading-driven site missions — candidate v2

Status: **historical candidate; superseded by
`TEN_SITE_DETAILED_STORY_ARCS_2026-08-15.md`**

The 2026-08-15 detailed story-flow packet controls where it conflicts with this
document. This earlier candidate remains only as review history.

This document supersedes the interactive-investigation structure in
`TEN_SITE_PLOT_CONTRACTS_2026-08-14.md`. The researched lessons and factual
guardrails remain useful, but the player no longer solves a complex puzzle on the
site canvas.

All ten missions use the approved structural boundaries:

- 8–12 passages, with a deliberately site-specific total and midpoint;
- right-hand Reading Companion as the primary interaction;
- left-hand site as layered visual progress and story reward;
- first obvious site click at the roughly halfway midpoint;
- Amy explains, Chinmay offers a sincere AI fix, and the fix triggers Phase 2;
- Amy's midpoint explanation introduces however many endgame repairs the lesson
  actually needs; no universal three-lock template;
- the midpoint cutscene occurs between passages and does not consume one; the
  first post-midpoint passage always produces a real Phase 2 visual change;
- reading metrics never gate progress and rereading is always optional;
- Finn is never shown; Techno stays anchored with lightweight reusable motion.

## 1. WikiWhy — The Article That Hated “Maybe”

**Lesson:** A collaborative encyclopedia is a useful starting point, but user-
generated claims need citations that actually support their wording.

**Opening:** A Wikipedia-like article declares `DOGS SEE ONLY BLACK AND WHITE —
100% CONFIRMED`. Qualifiers, citation markers, and edit history are missing.

### Passage sequence

| # | Reading form | Visual consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Original encyclopedia introduction to dog color vision | Removes the loudest vandalized banner. |
| 2 | Science explainer about cone cells and color range | Restores a simple color-spectrum graphic. |
| 3 | Research-summary paragraph | Returns one cautious sentence. |
| 4 | Data-rich comparison caption | Replaces a fake certainty meter with a labeled chart. |
| 5 | Vivid narrative about an early animal-vision experiment | Restores date and author context. |
| 6 | Two source excerpts using different certainty | Reveals missing citation sockets. |
| 7 | Editorial guide to `may`, `often`, and `evidence suggests` | Restores careful wording on one visible claim after the re-corruption. |
| 8 | Bibliographic record paired with the exact claim | Locks `Source actually supports it`. |
| 9 | Fictional edit-history log | Locks `Show what changed`. |
| 10 | Accurate synthesis of known and uncertain facts | Locks `Say what we still don't know` and secures the page. |

**Midpoint click:** `SHOW THE SOURCES`.

**Amy:** “The page sounds better, but we still need to see which source supports
each sentence.”

**Chinmay:** “Easy. My AI can reconnect everything—and remove the last few maybes
while it’s here.”

**Phase 2:** Citations snap onto the wrong claims, absolute wording returns, and
edit history hides behind `TRUST ME`.

**Endgame repairs:** `Source actually supports it / Show what changed / Say what
we still don't know`.

**Secured payoff:** The useful overview remains, major claims visibly connect to
evidence, and uncertainty is allowed.

**Minimal canvas:** One article base, Phase 1 cleanup overlays, one Phase 2
mis-citation swap, three repair overlays, one secured layer. Citation lines are
fixed authored overlays, never draggable connections.

**Techno:** Anchored beside the desktop; a head tilt at the mismatched citation,
then the ordinary idle loop.

## 2. ThreadIt — The Tater-Tot Robot Uprising

**Lesson:** Echo chambers can make repetition, votes, and apparent agreement feel
like confirmation even when they add no independent evidence.

**Opening:** A dense thread claims the cafeteria's new robot lunch cart has become
sentient and is hoarding tater tots under the bleachers. Ten dramatic accounts all
repeat one cropped three-second clip; the original upload, school schedule, full
video, and genuinely new observations are buried.

### Passage sequence

| # | Reading form | Visual consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Original dramatic robot-cart post | Restores the buried question header. |
| 2 | Three reposts with escalating captions | Identifies the repeated claim without declaring it true or false. |
| 3 | School schedule and cafeteria delivery note | Reveals a second kind of information. |
| 4 | Timestamp log | Groups impossibly fast reposts. |
| 5 | Three captions using the same cropped clip | Reduces duplicate visual noise. |
| 6 | Full clip transcript plus repost chain | Breaks the giant consensus badge into visible source groups. |
| 7 | Upload-origin record | Locks `First post`. |
| 8 | Copy comparison and a low-vote firsthand update | Locks `Copies` and restores `New independent information`. |
| 9 | Current school facilities note addressing the cart | Locks `Best available update` and secures the thread without claiming every mystery is solved. |

**Midpoint click:** `TRACE THIS POST`.

**Amy:** “These comments look like a crowd, but most lead back to the same post.
Agreement isn’t the same as new evidence.”

**Chinmay:** “Got it. My AI will combine all the votes into one extremely reliable
robot witness.”

**Phase 2:** Copied votes merge into `CONFIRMED BY THE COMMUNITY`, disagreement
collapses, and the low-vote new update disappears.

**Endgame repairs:** `First post / Copies / New independent information / Best
available update`.

**Secured payoff:** ThreadIt remains social, funny, and useful while clearly
separating popular repetition from genuinely new reporting. The school note is
especially relevant to the cart, not a claim that official sources are infallible;
one harmless tater-tot mystery may remain.

**Minimal canvas:** Static three-branch forum composition, vote overlay, origin
lines, consensus corruption layer, four final labels.

**Techno:** Anchored at the thread boundary; a two-pose gag places her ball beside
several apparent branches, then beside their single common origin.

## 3. FacePlace — The Legendary Fish, Cropped

**Lesson:** A happy post may be real without representing someone’s whole day or
feelings. Do not use a curated feed as a score for another life—or your own.

**Opening:** A fictional peer posts a flattering, tightly cropped photo of one happy
catch. Forced perspective makes the fish look legendary and the day look like a
solo wilderness triumph. The feed turns that real good moment into `BEST DAY / BEST
FISHER / BEST LIFE`, and its comparison meter reaches `AVOCADO%`.

### Passage sequence

| # | Reading form | Visual consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Upbeat fictional fishing caption | Keeps the happy moment while removing one duplicate. |
| 2 | Fictional first-person account of the same outing | Reveals that a friend helped without making the post false. |
| 3 | Wider photo caption and a text about tangled gear and cleanup | Restores ordinary details beyond the crop. |
| 4 | Short story retelling the catch from two viewpoints | Expands the timeline and makes the selected frame visible. |
| 5 | Friendly conversation after the trip | Restores the wide photo beside the AI's new trophy-fish recrop. |
| 6 | Selected-examples explainer | Locks `One posted moment`. |
| 7 | Observation-versus-assumption passage | Locks `No whole-life inference`. |
| 8 | Reflective piece about true but incomplete moments | Locks `No comparison score` and secures the feed. |

**Midpoint click:** `EXPAND THE FRAME`.

**Amy:** “The happy picture can be completely real. It still isn’t the whole
day—and it definitely isn’t a score for everyone else.”

**Chinmay:** “No problem. My AI can widen the truth and make the fish even bigger.”

**Phase 2:** The AI recrops the real photo into increasingly impossible trophy
angles, duplicates polished variations, turns ordinary details into success
metrics, and boosts `AVOCADO%`.

**Endgame repairs:** `One posted moment / No whole-life inference / No comparison score`.

**Secured payoff:** The peer's genuine happy catch remains alongside the wider,
messier, collaborative day. The page stops treating one chosen slice as a ranking
of entire lives.

**Minimal canvas:** One feed backplate, fictional-peer fishing photo states,
repeated-card overlay, crop masks, absurd meter, expanded context layer, three
secured labels. “Expand” is a precomposed state swap, not interactive crop
manipulation.

**Techno:** Anchored just beyond the crop; becomes visible at midpoint and settles
into a seated idle.

## 4. MyCorner — The Million-Dollar Layover

**Lesson:** A polished profile, urgent story, and promise of more money later do not
make a money request trustworthy. Pause, check the story, send nothing, and involve
a trusted adult.

**Opening:** A wholly fictional profile claims its owner is stranded abroad after
the International Pancake Robotics Finals. It urgently promises Finn ten times more
money later if he sends a smaller amount now. The practice UI permanently states
`FICTIONAL EXERCISE / $0 SENT / NO PAYMENT AVAILABLE`.

### Passage sequence

| # | Reading form | Visual consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Fictional profile biography | Restores the claimed identity without endorsing it. |
| 2 | Account-history record | Reveals that the profile and its travel story appeared minutes ago. |
| 3 | Absurd urgent money message | Highlights the request and the promised return without enabling payment. |
| 4 | Travel schedule and public event note | Exposes contradictions in the stranded-abroad story. |
| 5 | Age-appropriate scam-safety procedure | Pins `Pause and send nothing`. |
| 6 | Comic story about a too-good-to-be-true exchange | Restores the impossible promise after the AI makes it look respectable. |
| 7 | Profile-history and timeline comparison | Locks `Check the story`. |
| 8 | Request-language comparison | Locks `Urgency plus a big promise`. |
| 9 | Trusted-adult and report recap | Locks `Send nothing / Tell someone` and secures the page. |

**Midpoint click:** `CHECK THE STORY`.

**Amy:** “The profile looks polished. The story is still asking you to act fast and
send money now because it promises even more money later.”

**Chinmay:** “I can fix that. My AI will make the traveler more stranded and the
future money more enormous.”

**Phase 2:** The AI adds a gold passport border, twelve fake urgency stamps, and a
`1,000% GUARANTEED` badge while preserving the impossible timeline and request.

**Endgame repairs:** `Check the story / Notice urgency plus the big promise / Send
nothing and tell a trusted adult`.

**Secured payoff:** The fictional profile is labeled `STORY DOES NOT CHECK OUT` and
isolated. `FICTIONAL EXERCISE / $0 SENT / NO PAYMENT AVAILABLE` stays visible. Amy
adds: “A fancy profile and a huge promise are not reasons to rush.”

**Minimal canvas:** One fictional profile, fixed travel/timeline panel, absurd badge
overlays, practice-message layer, and quarantine layer. No payment field, external
link, functional messaging, or real account UI.

**Techno:** Anchored on the desktop; briefly receives a fake title of `INTERNATIONAL
PANCAKE AMBASSADOR`, then returns to `INTERESTS: BALL`.

## 5. Yahuh Portal — The Moon Has Resigned

**Lesson:** A sensational headline is written to sell a story, not to summarize it
reliably. Read beyond it and check what the article, support, and latest update say.

**Opening:** A portal screams absurd fictional world news: `MOON RESIGNS FROM NIGHT
SHIFT`, `OCEAN NOW 40% SOUP`, and `PIGEONS DEMAND MAYORAL RECOUNT`. Headlines,
thumbnails, and alarm meters overwhelm the quieter article text and corrections.

### Passage sequence

| # | Reading form | Visual consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Absurd headline paired with its opening paragraph | Separates the headline from the story body. |
| 2 | Photo caption and article excerpt | Shrinks one misleading thumbnail alarm. |
| 3 | Quoted fictional witness in full context | Restores words omitted by the headline. |
| 4 | Chronological update and correction note | Reveals that the story changed after publication. |
| 5 | Neutral summary of one fictional world event | Restores a readable story card after the AI makes every headline louder. |
| 6 | Headline-versus-body comparison | Locks `Read past the headline`. |
| 7 | Claim and support comparison | Locks `What supports it?`. |
| 8 | Update and correction comparison | Locks `What changed?`. |
| 9 | Comic synthesis of the portal's three absurd stories | Secures the portal without removing humor. |

**Midpoint click:** `READ PAST THE HEADLINE`.

**Amy:** “The headline is selling the story in five loud words. We still need to
see what the story actually says and what supports it.”

**Chinmay:** “Perfect. My AI will make the headlines so clear nobody has to read the
stories at all.”

**Phase 2:** The AI discards article nuance and rewrites every headline as a flashing
global emergency, adds unsupported exclamation marks, and promotes an ad-shaped
headline because it predicts more clicks.

**Endgame repairs:** `Read past the headline / What supports it? / What changed?`.

**Secured payoff:** The portal keeps funny, interesting headlines but places each
beside an accurate summary, visible support, and its latest correction. A headline
may still be playful; it no longer gets to substitute for the story.

**Minimal canvas:** One portal base, three authored absurd-news cards, headline/body
state swaps, alarm overlays, correction strips, and three repair indicators. No
functional tabs, live news feed, or external links.

**Techno:** Anchored at the edge; the portal briefly reports `LOCAL ORB REFUSES TO
COMMENT` beside her ball before returning it to a toy idle.

## 6. ViewTube — The Queue That Wouldn’t End

**Lesson:** Recommendations can be useful, but the algorithm choosing more video
is not necessarily serving Finn's goal. More content is not always better.

**Opening:** Finn arrives for one eight-minute project explainer. `UP NEXT` has
swollen into an endless tunnel, and its total runtime exceeds his available time.

### Passage sequence

| # | Reading form | Visual consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Original transcript from the sought explainer | Pins Finn's viewing goal. |
| 2 | Explanation of a viewer goal versus a prediction | Pauses one countdown layer. |
| 3 | Schedule showing twenty available minutes | Restores a total-time display. |
| 4 | Creator biography plus interface guide to autoplay | Restores the creator card and exposes the autoplay control. |
| 5 | Comic narrative plus videos serving the platform's incentives and Finn's interests | Collapses the all-day queue into a visible choice. |
| 6 | Viewing-time log | Locks `My viewing goal`. |
| 7 | Creator note about useful versus merely next | Locks `My time limit`. |
| 8 | Reflection on stopping intentionally and choosing a hobby video | Locks `Autoplay: my choice` and secures the queue. |

**Midpoint click:** `PAUSE AUTOPLAY`.

**Amy:** “The suggestions might be interesting. But they’re choosing what comes
next, not checking what you came here to do.”

**Chinmay:** “I’ll ask the AI for even more perfect recommendations. One of them
has to be right.”

**Phase 2:** `PERFECT FOR FINN` produces a queue longer than the rest of the day,
including `THIS VIDEO HAS THE MOST ADS`, `PART 1 OF 83`, and `WE PUT AN AD INSIDE AN
AD`, all presented as if platform incentives were Finn's interests.

**Endgame repairs:** `My viewing goal / My time limit / Autoplay: my choice`.

**Secured payoff:** `SELECTED BY FINN` replaces `SELECTED FOR FINN`. After finishing
the useful explainer, Finn deliberately chooses `MAKE A TEN-SECOND STOP-MOTION
TECHNO MOVIE`, a video tied to his own hobby rather than the queue's loudest
incentive.

**Minimal canvas:** Three watch-page backplates plus fixed countdown values,
runtime, queue, and repair overlays. No timer, playable video, carousel, or
scrolling feed.

**Techno:** Anchored beside the player controls; a paw pose at autoplay, then idle.

**Platform decision:** Keep ViewTube. Its discrete queue communicates the lesson
cleanly and fits the retro campaign better than a TikTok/Stories replacement.

## 7. Search-ish — The Answer With No Address

must fit the exact question.

**Opening:** A polished answer says “universal” glue is safe for Finn's foam-board

compatibility sheet, and careful test note.

| 2 | Fictional sponsored listing | Restores the ad label and sales purpose. |
| 3 | Dated hobby anecdote | Restores date and personal-experience context. |
| 4 | Original compatibility table | Restores structured material evidence. |
| 5 | Science explainer on solvents and foam | Exposes why broad claims are risky. |
| 6 | Dated test note using manufacturer safety guidance | Splits the AI mash-up back into separate source cards. |
| 7 | Byline/about-page passage | Locks `Who made this?`. |
| 8 | Publication-date and sales-purpose comparison | Locks `Why was it posted?`. |
| 9 | Evidence matched to the exact material question | Locks `Does it answer this question?`. |
| 10 | Research note with a supported answer and remaining uncertainty | Locks `What is still unknown?` and secures the results. |

**Midpoint click:** `SHOW WHERE THESE CAME FROM`.

**Amy:** “The top result can help. First we need to know who made it, why, and
whether it answers your actual question.”

**Chinmay:** “My AI will combine every result into one extremely sourced answer.”

**Phase 2:** The AI stitches incompatible sentences from the ad, old post, test
note, and compatibility sheet into one authoritative paragraph.

**Endgame repairs:** `Who made this? / Why was it posted? / Does it answer this
question? / What is still unknown?`.

**Secured payoff:** A fit-for-purpose source and small test support a cautious
answer; sponsored results remain useful leads rather than automatic truth or lies.
The passage directs Finn to follow the product label or manufacturer compatibility
guidance and ask an adult before testing an unfamiliar adhesive.

**Minimal canvas:** Three results-page backplates, metadata overlays, one source

**Techno:** Anchored near the desktop edge; briefly guards the foam-board test.

## 8. Amaze-On — The Fastest Bargain Makes the Biggest Pile

**Lesson:** Sponsored placement, speed, and low sticker price can encourage
instant gratification. Check product claims, useful life, and avoidable waste.

**Opening:** Finn needs a desk-height tripod under $25 for a stop-motion Techno
project. A tiny sponsored stand is `FASTEST / CHEAPEST / BEST`, despite dimensions
that do not meet the brief. Replacement products and packaging pile up behind the
cart.

### Passage sequence

| # | Reading form | Visual consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Project requirements and budget | Pins the real need. |
| 2 | Sponsored listing with fictional claims | Restores sponsorship labeling. |
| 3 | Dimensions/specification card | Reveals the height mismatch. |
| 4 | Measured test plus customer account separating opinion and defect | Removes `BEST` and restores a checkable issue. |
| 5 | Narrative about instant-delivery excitement | Slows the impulse countdown. |
| 6 | School packaging and disposal audit | Makes the replacement pile's waste legible. |
| 7 | Durability and repair guide | Restores the details hidden by the AI's larger badges. |
| 8 | Product claims compared with dimensions and test results | Locks `Do the claims match?`. |
| 9 | New/used/borrowed/repair comparison | Locks `Will it work and last?`. |
| 10 | Sticker price, replacement cost, and packaging comparison | Locks `What gets thrown away?`. |
| 11 | Budget decision recap with several valid outcomes | Locks `The choice stays with Finn` and secures the cart. |

**Midpoint click:** `COMPARE FULL DETAILS`.

**Amy:** “Fast and cheap might be right—but only if the product matches the claim
and you won’t need to replace it immediately.”

**Chinmay:** “The AI will find something cheaper, faster, greener, and somehow
already at the door.”

**Phase 2:** Dimensions vanish, unsupported `ECO` and `BEST VALUE` badges expand,
and one bad match becomes a comic avalanche of replacements and packaging.

**Endgame repairs:** `Do the claims match? / Will it work and last? / What gets
thrown away? / The choice stays with Finn`.

**Secured payoff:** Borrowing, choosing a durable in-budget product, repairing, or
`Buy nothing yet` can all be responsible depending on the need. The practice cart
always shows `0 ORDERS / $0 CHARGED` until separate confirmation.

**Minimal canvas:** Three storefront backplates, two or three fixed package-pile
overlays, badge layers, and four repair indicators. No checkout, branching purchase
logic, or sortable catalog. The fictional `ECO` claim is unsupported because its
basis is visibly missing; the game does not generalize that fast or cheap goods
always create more waste.

**Techno:** Anchored beside the package pile; ends with room for her ball again.

tracks with altered names multiply until her work disappears beneath
`PERFECTLY GENERATED FOR YOU`.

### Passage sequence

| 1 | Local-show flyer and short artist biography | Restores her name and profile silhouette. |
| 2 | Personal program note explaining one creative decision | Restores the song's purpose. |
| 3 | Zine-style review plus short artist interview | Restores the artist as a person, not audio evidence. |
| 4 | Annotated track-credit card | Returns contributor names. |
| 5 | Artist statement about permitted and unpermitted uses | Restores provenance context. |
| 6 | Another artist describing consensual AI assistance | Restores one clearly disclosed AI-assisted creator beside the opaque flood. |
| 7 | Playlist caption about discovering the local show | Locks `Who made it?`. |
| 8 | Annotated record of a distinctive creative choice | Locks `What did they create?`. |
| 9 | Artist-supplied permission statement | Locks `Did they agree?`. |
| 10 | Reflection on discovery and following creators | Locks `Show the credit` and secures the library. |

**Midpoint click:** `SHOW FULL CREDITS`.

**Amy:** “You don’t have to guess from the sound. Credits and the creator’s own

**Chinmay:** “Credits take up valuable music space. My AI can make one perfect

**Phase 2:** Every profile merges into `ARTIST: EVERYONE`; endless generated tracks
fill the shelf, contributors vanish, and the original creator moves off-screen.

**Endgame repairs:** `Who made it? / What did they create? / Did they agree? /

**Techno:** Anchored near the queue; recognizes a documented squeaky-ball sample

## 10. MapGuess — The Confident Route to Somewhere Else

**Lesson:** A confident route needs fixed anchors. Confirm the destination, road,
and landmark; keep paid suggestions separate from the chosen goal.

**Opening:** While planning at home for Amy's library reservation, Finn sees a
`2 MINUTES` route across a lake ending at a sponsored snack shop instead of the
library. This is explicitly the fictional AI's corruption.

### Passage sequence

| # | Reading form | Visual consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Amy's reservation note | Restores the chosen destination name. |
| 2 | Library card with street address | Restores the address. |
| 3 | Map-legend passage | Makes roads, water, and bridge legible. |
| 4 | Current bridge notice and route photo caption | Restores the bridge label and exposes the water crossing. |
| 5 | Walking timetable plus clock-tower description | Snaps the fixed bridge and landmark back over the AI's moved destination. |
| 6 | Route comparison | Locks `Destination`. |
| 7 | Sponsored-place description plus pre-trip safety note | Locks `Route`. |
| 8 | Final itinerary | Locks `Landmark` and secures the map. |

**Midpoint click:** `LOCK MY DESTINATION`.

**Amy:** “The estimate sounds certain, but the address, bridge, and landmark all
have to agree with it.”

**Chinmay:** “My AI can make the route correct by moving the destination closer.”

**Phase 2:** The library pin slides onto the sponsored snack shop while the road,
label, and `2 MINUTES` pretend nothing changed.

**Endgame repairs:** `Destination / Route / Landmark`.

**Secured payoff:** The path follows the bridge to the library. The sponsored shop
remains a harmless labeled suggestion, separate from Finn's chosen destination.
The pre-trip passage reminds Finn to check current conditions before leaving and
ask an adult or local authority when a route still looks unsafe or impossible.

**Minimal canvas:** Three map backplates, two authored pin positions, baked route
strokes, sponsor marker, three anchor overlays, one destination button. No pan,
zoom, geolocation, pathfinding, or turn-by-turn navigation.

**Techno:** Anchored beside the map; places her ball by the stable destination in
the final pose.

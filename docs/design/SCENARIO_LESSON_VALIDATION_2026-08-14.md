# Ten-site scenario–lesson validation — live-review revision

Status: **historical validation pass; superseded by
`TEN_SITE_DETAILED_STORY_ARCS_2026-08-15.md`; no boards or code authorized**

This pass applies Chinmay's live feedback and tests whether each scenario can carry
both its platform-specific lesson and the campaign's broader story about a
well-intentioned human repeatedly giving an AI the wrong priorities. The tonal bar
is funny, playful absurdism with mad-scientist energy for a fourteen-year-old—not
grounded realism and not grand mythic seriousness.

## 1. WikiWhy — The Article That Hated “Maybe”

**Lesson:** A collaborative encyclopedia is useful, but user-generated claims need
citations that actually support their wording, with uncertainty and edit context
left visible.

**Scenario:** One dog-color-vision article escalates from `DOGS SEE ONLY BLACK AND
WHITE—100% CONFIRMED` into a citation tangle after Chinmay asks the AI to eliminate
the maybes.

**Why it earns the lesson:** The topic supports scientific explanation, historical
claims, charts, source matching, cautious wording, and edit history without changing
subjects. The AI's desire for certainty creates the site-specific failure and also
advances the campaign's misaligned-priorities story.

**Arc:** Initial corruption strips qualifiers, sources, and edit history; Phase 1
restores a more accurate spectrum and exposes empty citation sockets; at midpoint
Chinmay reconnects everything with AI; Phase 2 confidently attaches the wrong
sources and hides uncertainty; the secured state shows supported claims, visible
changes, and honest unknowns while keeping the article useful.

**Remaining material decision:** None now. Dog color vision has provisional approval;
before board freeze, validate that the actual storyboard makes this campaign-level
progression visible rather than merely decorating an article.

## 2. ThreadIt — The Tater-Tot Robot Uprising

**Lesson:** Echo chambers can make repetition, votes, and apparent agreement feel
like confirmation even when they add no independent evidence.

**Scenario:** A school thread claims the cafeteria's robot lunch cart has become
sentient and is hoarding tater tots under the bleachers. Ten dramatic accounts turn
out to repeat one cropped three-second clip, while a schedule, full clip, facilities
note, and low-vote firsthand update carry genuinely different information.

**Why it earns the lesson:** The mystery has multiple evidence types, altered
retellings, timestamps, popularity signals, and a conclusion that can remain partly
open. It is comic enough to sustain nine visual beats, and Chinmay's consensus AI
can visibly mistake ten copies for ten witnesses.

**Arc:** Initial corruption presents a roaring crowd and ten apparent branches;
Phase 1 exposes copy clusters and the buried original; at midpoint Chinmay combines
the votes into one “extremely reliable robot witness”; Phase 2 collapses all copies
into `CONFIRMED BY THE COMMUNITY` and hides the new update; the secured state maps
the single origin, copies, genuinely new information, and best current update while
leaving one harmless tater-tot mystery.

**Remaining material decision:** Approve or replace the robot-cart rumor before the
detailed nine-passage copy and board composition are frozen.

## 3. FacePlace — The Legendary Fish, Cropped

**Lesson:** A happy post can be true without representing someone's whole day; a
chosen slice should not become a score for that person's life or anyone else's.

**Scenario:** A fictional peer posts a flattering fishing photo. The tight crop and
forced perspective make a real happy catch look like a giant solo wilderness
triumph; the wider frame reveals the friend who helped, ordinary-sized fish, tangled
gear, cleanup, and a crowded fishing spot without invalidating the joy.

**Why it earns the lesson:** The same moment supports two impressions without a
cheap “the post was a lie” reveal. Crop, timeline, viewpoint, comparison metrics,
and AI-generated trophy angles provide enough distinct material for both phases.

**Arc:** Initial corruption duplicates the crop and turns it into `BEST DAY / BEST
FISHER / BEST LIFE`; Phase 1 preserves the happy post while revealing clues outside
the frame; at midpoint Finn expands the frame; Chinmay then asks AI to “widen the
truth”; Phase 2 produces increasingly impossible trophy crops and a rising
`AVOCADO%` score; the secured state places the happy catch beside the wider, messier,
collaborative day and removes whole-life ranking.

**Remaining material decision:** The fictional-peer fishing premise is directed.
The exact wider-frame context—currently proposed as help, ordinary scale, tangled
gear, cleanup, and a busier setting—remains open for the later substantive review.

## 4. MyCorner — The Million-Dollar Layover

**Lesson:** A polished profile, urgent story, and promise of more money later do not
make a money request trustworthy. Pause, check the story, send nothing, and involve
a trusted adult.

**Scenario:** A wholly fictional profile claims its owner is stranded abroad after
the International Pancake Robotics Finals. It urgently promises Finn ten times more
money later if he sends a smaller amount now. The simulation permanently states
`FICTIONAL EXERCISE / $0 SENT / NO PAYMENT AVAILABLE`.

**Why it earns the lesson:** The scenario supports profile history, timeline
contradictions, urgency language, an impossible return, safe refusal, trusted-adult
help, and reporting without using a real identity or payment flow. Chinmay's AI can
make every trust signal shinier while making the core promise even more absurd.

**Arc:** Initial corruption lets the glossy request dominate the profile; Phase 1
restores the new-account timeline, travel contradiction, and too-good-to-be-true
promise; at midpoint Finn checks the story; Chinmay asks AI to make the traveler more
stranded and the future money larger; Phase 2 adds a gold passport border, fake
urgency stamps, and `1,000% GUARANTEED`; the secured state labels the story unchecked,
isolates the profile, sends nothing, and points to a trusted adult.

**Remaining material decision:** None at scenario level. The fictional advance-fee
parody and no-real-payment boundary are directed; final wording still needs youth-
safety and comprehension review.

## 5. Yahuh Portal — The Moon Has Resigned

**Lesson:** A sensational headline is written to sell a story, not to summarize it
reliably. Read beyond it and check what the article, support, and latest update say.

**Scenario:** The portal screams absurd fictional world news: `MOON RESIGNS FROM
NIGHT SHIFT`, `OCEAN NOW 40% SOUP`, and `PIGEONS DEMAND MAYORAL RECOUNT`. Headlines,
thumbnails, ads, and alarm meters overwhelm quieter story text and corrections.

**Why it earns the lesson:** Multiple fictional events let the mission compare a
headline with its body, caption, quotation, support, chronology, and correction
without drifting into personalized-feed design. Chinmay's AI has a clear campaign
mistake: it treats predicted clicks as proof that a headline communicates well.

**Arc:** Initial corruption makes every event a flashing emergency; Phase 1 restores
article bodies, quote context, and correction strips; at midpoint Finn reads past a
headline; Chinmay asks AI to make headlines so clear nobody needs the stories; Phase
2 discards nuance, adds unsupported exclamation marks, and promotes an ad-shaped
headline; the secured state keeps playful headlines beside accurate summaries,
visible support, and current updates.

**Remaining material decision:** None at lesson level. The exact absurd events and
headlines are provisional copy choices for the later substantive review.

## 6. ViewTube — The Queue That Wouldn't End

**Lesson:** Recommendations may be useful, but the system choosing more video is not
necessarily serving Finn's goal; more content is not always better.

**Scenario:** Finn arrives for one eight-minute project explainer with twenty minutes
available. The next-video queue grows from plausible tutorials into an all-day
tunnel whose blunt titles expose conflicting incentives: `THIS VIDEO HAS THE MOST
ADS`, `PART 1 OF 83`, and `WE PUT AN AD INSIDE AN AD`.

**Why it earns the lesson:** A concrete goal and time boundary make recommendation
quality distinct from endless relevance. The queue, total runtime, creator context,
autoplay state, and stopping choice provide a legible escalation without requiring
video playback or a second lesson about source truth.

**Arc:** Initial corruption buries the intended explainer beneath an endless queue;
Phase 1 restores Finn's goal, time limit, and autoplay control; at midpoint Finn
pauses autoplay; Chinmay asks for even more perfect recommendations; Phase 2 expands
`PERFECT FOR FINN` beyond the rest of the day and exposes the platform-serving
titles; the secured state changes `SELECTED FOR FINN` to `SELECTED BY FINN`, finishes
the useful explainer, and lets Finn deliberately choose `MAKE A TEN-SECOND
STOP-MOTION TECHNO MOVIE` because it matches his hobby.

**Remaining material decision:** None at scenario level. The hobby-choice ending and
blunt incentive-parody titles are directed; final title wording remains provisional.

## 7. Search-ish — The Answer With No Address

**Lesson:** Search results are leads; a useful source needs a traceable origin and
must fit the exact question.

**Scenario:** Finn needs adhesive for a foam-board build. A polished answer calls one
glue “universal,” while the result set mixes a sponsored listing, an old hobby
anecdote, a current compatibility sheet, a material-science explanation, and a
careful test note.

**Why it earns the lesson:** One practical question lets the mission compare who
made each source, why it exists, when it was written, and whether it answers this
exact material problem. The evidence can disagree for understandable reasons rather
than forming a simplistic true/false pile.

**Arc:** Initial corruption promotes the confident universal answer and erases
metadata; Phase 1 restores source boundaries and the exact project constraint; at
midpoint Finn asks where the claims came from; Chinmay combines every result into one
“extremely sourced” paragraph; Phase 2 mashes incompatible claims together; the
secured state separates origin, purpose, fit, and remaining uncertainty and supports
a cautious answer.

**Remaining material decision:** Confirm the foam-board adhesive build as the final
anchor before producing result cards and product/source imagery.

## 8. Amaze-On — The Fastest Bargain Makes the Biggest Pile

**Lesson:** Sponsored placement, speed, low price, and unsupported claims can reward
instant gratification while hiding poor fit, short useful life, and avoidable waste.

**Scenario:** Finn needs a desk-height tripod under $25 for a stop-motion Techno
project. The fastest sponsored stand is tiny, overclaims its strength and greenness,
and spawns an absurd pile of replacements and packaging when treated as the obvious
choice.

**Why it earns the lesson:** A specific need makes dimensions, claims, durability,
replacement cost, packaging, repair, borrowing, and waiting comparable without
invoking labor-supply-chain ethics. The stop-motion goal supplies visual personality,
and the package avalanche gives the environmental consequence comic momentum.

**Arc:** Initial corruption crowns the tiny stand `FASTEST / CHEAPEST / BEST`; Phase
1 restores the brief, dimensions, test evidence, and first visible waste; at midpoint
Finn compares full details; Chinmay asks AI for something cheaper, faster, greener,
and already at the door; Phase 2 erases dimensions and triggers a fixed avalanche of
bad replacements; the secured state checks claims, useful life, and waste while
leaving the purchase choice with Finn and charging nothing.

**Remaining material decision:** Confirm the stop-motion tripod as the product
scenario before product art and packaging states are designed.

## 9. Spotty-Fi — The Playlist With No One In It

**Lesson:** When opaque generated content floods discovery, distinctive individual
creators can become harder to find and support; visible authorship, contribution,
permission, and credit matter.

**Scenario:** Finn searches for a local teen artist he enjoyed live. Near-identical
tracks with altered names multiply until the artist, her collaborators, and even one
clearly disclosed consensual AI-assisted work disappear beneath `PERFECTLY GENERATED
FOR YOU`.

**Why it earns the lesson:** The mission can use a flyer, artist note, interview,
credits, permission statement, playlist caption, and disclosed counterexample. That
supports a richer creator story than merely spotting a fake song and preserves the
distinction between consensual assistance and opaque displacement.

**Arc:** Initial corruption floods discovery and strips creator identity; Phase 1
restores the local artist's name, choices, collaborators, and statement; at midpoint
Finn opens full credits; Chinmay removes “wasted music space” by asking AI for one
perfect artist; Phase 2 merges everyone into `ARTIST: EVERYONE`; the secured state
returns named creators, their contributions, permission status, and visible credit
without asking Finn to judge legality or copying by ear.

**Remaining material decision:** Choose whether the emotional center remains one
local artist being displaced or broadens to several creators; one artist gives the
stronger plot, while the generated flood can still visualize the systemic scale.

## 10. MapGuess — The Confident Route to Somewhere Else

**Lesson:** A confident route needs fixed anchors, and sponsored suggestions must
remain separate from the destination the user chose.

**Scenario:** While planning at home for Amy's library reservation, Finn receives a
confident two-minute route across a lake that ends at a sponsored snack shop. The
library address, current bridge notice, clock-tower landmark, and timetable disagree
with the polished route.

**Why it earns the lesson:** Destination, address, road, bridge, landmark, timing,
and sponsorship give the route several independent anchors. The absurd lake crossing
and sliding destination pin make the AI's “fix” funny and visual while keeping the
lesson about confidence and user intent.

**Arc:** Initial corruption confidently routes to the wrong place across water;
Phase 1 restores the library name, address, bridge, and landmark; at midpoint Finn
locks the destination; Chinmay asks AI to fix the route by moving the destination;
Phase 2 slides the library pin onto the sponsored snack shop while every label claims
nothing changed; the secured state aligns destination, bridge route, and landmark and
leaves the snack shop as a harmless labeled suggestion.

**Remaining material decision:** Confirm the library reservation and sponsored snack
shop as the final destination pair before map art is composed.

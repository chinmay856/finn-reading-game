## Editorial rule

All 92 campaign passages should be built from authored public-domain literature, essays,
memoirs, criticism, journalism, historical documents, or science. The game may
add a short factual source introduction, comprehension question, and vocabulary
cards, but it should not substitute project-written prose or a paraphrase-heavy
modern synthesis for the work itself.

The default packet is one self-contained excerpt from one work. Paired-source
synthesis is an occasional comparative-reading format, not the campaign's
dominant form.

Site mechanics and passage subjects need only share a broad theme. A passage is
selected first because it is rewarding, well written, and suitable for reading
aloud; it does not need to narrate the precise repair shown on screen.

### Permitted paired-source synthesis

A synthesis may combine two attributed public-domain excerpts when the contrast
is itself worth reading. In that format:

- roughly 70–85% of the scored packet should remain verbatim source text;
- each excerpt should be visibly and audibly introduced with author and work;
- a short editorial bridge may explain the question, agreement, or contrast;
- quotation boundaries must remain explicit rather than blending two authors
  into one generated voice;
- the comprehension question may ask the reader to compare the authors' claims,
  evidence, assumptions, or definitions;
- vocabulary must come from the quoted passages, not the editorial bridge.

This is positive generated content because it curates and connects original
writing while preserving authorship, wording, and attribution.

### Spoken source introduction

Keep attribution to one short scored sentence containing the work and author.
The default pattern is:

> An excerpt from “How to Tell a Story” by Mark Twain.

Form, genre, chapter, edition, rights, and historical context remain available
as metadata but should not become a second spoken sentence unless the reader
genuinely needs a few words of context to understand the excerpt. Avoid the
repetitive two-sentence pattern “This is an excerpt … It is …”. If the form is
useful, fold it into the same short line, for example:

> A scientific excerpt titled “On the Method of Zadig” by T. H. Huxley.

## Production gates

- Select self-contained excerpts of roughly 275–325 spoken words.
- Preserve authentic wording exactly; do not simplify or paraphrase the excerpt.
- For paired-source packets, keep the quoted source material dominant and label
  every transition between source text and editorial framing.
- Use one short spoken source-introduction sentence. It must name the work and
  author; include form or context only when it materially helps comprehension.
- Use the original passage, not a generated summary, as the scored reading text.
- Freeze edition, chapter or section, source URL, retrieval date, U.S. public-domain
  evidence, and any translation metadata.
- Add one text-supported three-choice comprehension question and three
  context-correct vocabulary words after the excerpt is frozen.
- Review archaic diction, sentence length, historical sensitivity, and microphone
  behavior without silently rewriting the source.
- Treat readability formulas as flags. A difficult passage may remain as a
  supported stretch reading if it is interesting and coherent aloud.

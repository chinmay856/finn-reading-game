# Alice's Evidence — approved Rule Forty-two expansion

Approved by Chinmay after the initial release on 2026-09-20. The exact addition below is incorporated in WIKIWHY_HUMAN_REVIEWED_PACKET_RULE_42.md (340 total words). Earlier proposal rationale follows as history.

The current live passage is 257 spoken words, including source introduction,
speaker names, and the two written transitions. It is already similar in length
to the 243-word Time Machine passage; the short exchanges make it feel quicker.

Recommendation: if a longer Alice reading is wanted, prepend the Rule Forty-two
exchange immediately before the existing “Consider your verdict.” It supplies
another concrete example of invented authority, adds a joke Alice can challenge,
and flows directly into the existing passage. The vocabulary and quick check can
stay focused on the letter. This is an optional editorial proposal, not a change
to the live game or its playtester Doc.

Source: Lewis Carroll, *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland*, chapter XII,
[Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/11/pg11-images.html#chap12).
Public domain in the USA. Dialogue below retains source wording with typography
normalized; stage directions are project-written for the existing script format.

Insert after the source introduction:

*During the trial, the King reads a rule from his notebook. Everyone looks at Alice.*

**King:** “Rule Forty-two. All persons more than a mile high to leave the court.”

**Alice:** “I'm not a mile high.”

**King:** “You are.”

**Queen:** “Nearly two miles high.”

**Alice:** “Well, I shan't go, at any rate: besides, that's not a regular rule: you invented it just now.”

**King:** “It's the oldest rule in the book.”

**Alice:** “Then it ought to be Number One.”

*The King closes his notebook.*

Continue with the existing **King:** “Consider your verdict.”

An alternative is to restore the omitted exchange in which the White Rabbit
unfolds the paper and discovers it is a set of verses rather than a letter.
That would strengthen context, but would also require changing the current
singular references and reviewing the question and distractors. Adding the full
nonsense poem would make the reading considerably longer and less focused;
the short Rule Forty-two addition is the cleaner first option.
